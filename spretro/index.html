    Supine retroperitoneal SP access via a low anterior incision

    A reproducible, low-morbidity approach to the ureter for single-port robotic reconstruction. The retroperitoneum is entered directly through a small incision near the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS), the ureter is localized with a ureteroscope under fluorescence, and the SP system is docked at low insufflation pressure.

    Before you start

    Equipment & room setup

    Have everything staged before the patient is positioned. The ureteroscope in particular must be available at the start of the case — it identifies the ureter and the level of the stricture, and later serves as the intracorporeal light source for fluorescence localization.

    Core equipment — tap to check off
    ✦
    PearlKeep the flexible ureteroscope draped in and connected throughout the case. Beyond mapping the stricture, its light tip is what you will chase with the robotic camera in fluorescence mode to find the ureter.
    01
    Positioning

    Position the patient & create the iliac bump

    Position depends on whether you will need urethral access during the reconstruction.

    • Supine, flat — the default for most mid- and proximal-ureteral work.
    • Low lithotomy — choose this when urethral or lower-tract access may be required (e.g. concurrent cystoscopy, distal ureteral reconstruction).
    • Place a small bump under the ipsilateral ASIS — a 1 L IV-fluid bag wrapped in a blanket works well — to open the space between the costal margin and the iliac crest and present the lower quadrant.
    • Tuck and pad the arms. Confirm all pressure points are protected before prepping.
    ✦
    PearlConfirm your position decision before the prep. Converting from flat to lithotomy after draping is disruptive and breaks sterility planning around the perineum.
    02
    Mapping the target

    Ureteroscopy, guidewire & catheter setup

    Begin endoscopically to define exactly where you are headed before any incision is made.

    • Perform flexible ureteroscopy and advance a guidewire and the ureteroscope up to the stenosis, confirming the level and length of the diseased segment.
    • No intra-ureteral ICG. The ureteroscope's own light — not dye — is used later for fluorescence localization.
    • Place a 14 Fr Foley alongside the ureteroscope so both occupy the urethra during mapping.
    • Plan for an 18 Fr Foley as the final catheter once the scope is removed.
    i
    Why scope firstLocalizing the stricture endoscopically lets you site the incision over the correct segment of ureter and keeps the scope in place as a homing beacon for the robotic camera.
    03
    Surface landmarks

    Mark the low anterior incision

    The incision is built off a single reliable landmark — the ipsilateral ASIS.

    • Identify and mark the ipsilateral ASIS.
    • Measure two finger-breadths above the ASIS.
    • Mark a 3 cm transverse incision, sited slightly medial to the midpoint of the ASIS.
    ✦
    PearlA 3 cm transverse incision is enough for the SP access port. Keeping it low and lateral places you directly over the retroperitoneal fat pad and away from the rectus sheath.
    04
    Open access

    Dissect through the abdominal wall, layer by layer

    Work down through the lateral abdominal wall one fascial layer at a time. Clear, place stay sutures, and divide each layer in turn. Step through the layers below to see each stage.

    Depth · superficial → deep
    Layer 1 · External oblique

    External oblique fascia

    Clear the fat off the external oblique aponeurosis, place stay sutures on either side, and incise it.

    !
    Pitfall — the #1 reason access failsIf the transversus abdominis fascia is not divided, the retroperitoneum will not be entered. It is frequently thin and easy to mistake for areolar tissue — confirm you have divided it before sweeping.
    ✦
    Tip — peritoneotomy rescueIf the peritoneum is inadvertently entered, it can simply be closed with a suture and the operation can proceed as planned. A small peritoneal opening is not a reason to abort the retroperitoneal approach.
    05
    Develop the space

    Enter the retroperitoneum & find the psoas

    With the transversalis breached, develop the retroperitoneal space and establish your floor.

    • Finger-sweep through the transversalis fascia toward the ipsilateral ASIS; hook the retroperitoneal fat and peritoneum and sweep them medially.
    • Place the small da Vinci SP "beach-ball" access port into the developed space.
    • Insufflate at low flow to 8 mmHg and identify the psoas fascia — this is the floor of the dissection and your orientation anchor.
    The psoas plane in practice
    i
    If a nephrostomy tube is presentConnect long yellow extension tubing to it, with a 3-way stopcock and a 50 cc Luer-lock syringe on the sterile field, so the collecting system can be flushed or distended on demand.
    06
    Custom instrument

    Build the custom suction-irrigator

    A single-port field has no dedicated suction-irrigator arm, so one is assembled on the back table. Connect the components in order, from the drain at the patient end to the Neptune at the suction end.

    Fluid path (drain → suction) Irrigation branch (manual, via syringe)
    ✦
    PearlCut the 15 Fr round Blake/Jackson-Pratt drain to length and tie 2-0 silk holding sutures as handles — they let the bedside assistant retrieve and reposition the tip through the single port without an extra instrument.
    07
    Robot setup

    Insufflate & dock the SP robot

    • Insufflate the retroperitoneum with low flow, AirSeal at 8 mmHg — enough to hold the space without over-distending the confined retroperitoneal cavity.
    • Dock the robot to the SP access port and bring in the camera and instruments.
    !
    PitfallResist the urge to raise pressure. The retroperitoneum is a small, contained space — higher insufflation buys little working room and increases the risk of collapsing the working cavity or losing CO₂ through the open peritoneal sweep.
    08
    Fluorescence localization

    Find the ureter with the ureteroscope light

    Now the ureteroscope earns its keep. Switch the robotic camera into fluorescence (Firefly) mode and look for the glow of the ureteroscope's light shining through the wall of the ureter — no ICG required.

    Toggle the camera mode. In standard view the anatomy reads by color; in fluorescence the field darkens and the scope's transilluminated light marks the ureter mid-course.
